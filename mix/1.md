# Research Evidence Supporting Features

## 1. Capture a Response

### Probe Evidence

Interactive activities received the highest number of responses. This suggests that visitors prefer active participation rather than passive viewing.

### Interview Evidence

> “The best part was probably seeing my child actually engaged, especially when there was something hands-on to do instead of just walking around looking at artworks.”  
> — Elaris_3

> “If there’s something I can walk around, interact with, or respond to, it feels easier to get involved.”  
> — minngjie xi

### Literature Evidence

**Source:** _Protocol to Build a Framework for Designing an Interactive Tool for Art Gallery Visitors_

> “Gallery visitors are more akin to active explorers and meaning makers than passive receivers of content.”

This supports a feature that allows visitors to actively record their observations, feelings, and interpretations of artworks.

---

## 2. Shared Visitor Traces

### Probe Evidence

Ways to interact with others received a high number of responses, showing visitors’ interest in social interaction during gallery visits.

### Interview Evidence

> “After I finished my sketch, I kind of wished I had someone to show it to. It wasn't very good, but I still wanted to laugh about it with someone or see how they would draw the same thing.”  
> — Elaris_2

> “I kind of wanted to ask her for some tips, but... I don't know, I felt a bit awkward just talking to a stranger.”  
> — Elaris_2

### Literature Evidence

**Source:** _The Silent Conversation: Designing for Introspection and Social Play in Art Museums_

> “Players used the app for communicating with each other during the visit, often without speaking.”

This supports anonymous and non-verbal visitor traces that allow people to interact without directly speaking to strangers.

---

## 3. Gallery Map

### Probe Evidence

Clear routes and maps or guides received noticeable interest. One participant also suggested:

> “Visitor route recommendation.”

### Interview Evidence

> “The route wasn't super clear, so sometimes we ended up walking through the same area again.”  
> — Elaris_1

> “We kind of ended up doing loops through the same rooms because we couldn't tell if we'd already seen everything.”  
> — Elaris_3

### Literature Evidence

**Source:** _Flow, Staging, Wayfinding, Personalization: Evaluating User Experience with Mobile Museum Narratives_

The study highlights the importance of visitor control and flexible mobile guidance for different visitor needs.

This supports a gallery map that shows visited, unvisited, and temporarily skipped areas without forcing visitors to follow a fixed route.

---

## 4. My Collection

### Probe Evidence

Ways to save favourites received a high number of responses, showing that visitors want to keep meaningful content from their visits.

### Interview Evidence

> “We’d stop when one of us saw something interesting and talk about it a little. I also took a few photos, mostly of artworks that looked unusual or that I wanted to remember.”  
> — Participant 1, Interview Excerpt 1

> “After I finished my sketch, I kind of wished I had someone to show it to.”  
> — Elaris_2

### Literature Evidence

**Source:** _Sensitive Pictures: Emotional Interpretation in the Museum_

> “Presented these back to visitors as data souvenirs.”

The study suggests that data collected during a museum visit can become a personalised souvenir that supports reflection after the visit.

This supports My Collection, where visitors can save artworks, personal responses, and visual traces for later review.
