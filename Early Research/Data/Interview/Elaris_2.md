# Gallery Visitor Interview Transcript

**Participant Profile:** Casual visitor, visited alone on a whim, tried sketching with gallery-provided pencils.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time you visited an art gallery? Who were you with, and what did you do during the visit?

**Participant:** Um... yeah, I went to a gallery in the city last weekend. It was kind of spontaneous. I had a free afternoon and just wanted somewhere quiet to spend some time.

**Interviewer:** Did you go with anyone?

**Participant:** No, I went by myself.

**Interviewer:** What did you do while you were there?

**Participant:** Mostly just walked around and looked at the artworks. But then I noticed they had some paper and pencils for visitors, so I tried sketching for a bit. I sat down and tried to draw a statue for maybe twenty minutes. I also took a couple of photos of the artwork labels so I could look them up later.

**Interviewer:** Can you remember a moment when something made you stop for longer or pay more attention?

**Participant:** Hmm... yeah, there was this big landscape painting.

**Interviewer:** What caught your attention?

**Participant:** I sat down to sketch the shadows under the trees, and, I don't know, when you actually try to draw it, you start looking way closer. Once I started drawing, I realised the dark areas actually had lots of different strokes and layers. I probably wouldn't have noticed that if I was just walking past.

**Interviewer:** So sketching made you slow down a bit?

**Participant:** Yeah, definitely. You kind of have to look properly when you're trying to copy something.

**Interviewer:** When you visit with friends, does that change the way you experience the gallery?

**Participant:** Yeah... I think so. When I'm with friends, we talk a lot more and joke about things, so it's more social.

**Interviewer:** And when you're alone?

**Participant:** I can spend much longer on one thing. Like, I could sit there for twenty minutes just messing around with a pencil and not worry about making someone wait.

**Interviewer:** Can you think of a time when another visitor changed what you did or noticed?

**Participant:** Yeah, actually. When I was sketching, another person sat nearby and started drawing too.

**Interviewer:** Did you notice anything about what they were doing?

**Participant:** Her sketch looked really clean. Mine was... pretty rough. And the gallery only gave us pencils, no erasers, so I couldn't really fix anything.

**Interviewer:** Did watching her change the way you drew?

**Participant:** A little, yeah. I noticed she was pressing really lightly and building things up slowly. I was drawing way too hard. I kind of wanted to ask her for some tips, but... I don't know, I felt a bit awkward just talking to a stranger.

**Interviewer:** Are there times when you prefer being alone, and times when you want to share the experience with someone?

**Participant:** Yeah. When I'm just walking around, I actually prefer being alone because I can go at my own pace.

**Interviewer:** What about when you're sketching?

**Participant:** That's different. After I finished my sketch, I kind of wished I had someone to show it to. It wasn't very good, but I still wanted to laugh about it with someone or see how they would draw the same thing.

**Interviewer:** Why do you think that is?

**Participant:** I guess once you actually make something yourself, you want to share it a bit more.

**Interviewer:** Thinking back on the visit, what made it enjoyable or uncomfortable?

**Participant:** The sketching was probably the best part. Even though my drawing wasn't great, it made me remember the artwork much more clearly.

**Interviewer:** Was there anything uncomfortable?

**Participant:** Yeah... the bench wasn't very comfortable. It didn't have a backrest, so after leaning forward for twenty minutes, my back and neck were getting pretty stiff.

**Interviewer:** Anything else?

**Participant:** Probably the no-eraser thing, honestly. It was a little frustrating, but also kind of funny. Overall, the sketching made the visit feel a lot more memorable.
