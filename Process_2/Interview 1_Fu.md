# Interview 1

## 1. Can you briefly describe what you think Gallery Echoes is for, in your own words?

I think Gallery Echoes is designed to make viewing art more interactive and personal. It allows visitors to share their feelings and see how other people respond to the same artwork.

## 2. Was there any point during the experience where you were unsure what to do next? What happened?

I was briefly unsure about what to do after first opening the experience. I was not immediately sure whether I should tap the artwork, scroll, or look for another instruction. A clearer prompt at the beginning would make the process easier.

## 3. After tapping the NFC tag, did the steps from viewing the artwork to sharing your feelings make sense to you? Why or why not?

Yes, the process mostly made sense. I could view the artwork first and then share my feelings, so the main steps felt logical. However, the instructions could be slightly clearer about what to do after submitting a response.

## 4. What did you think the collective visual represented?

I thought the collective visual represented the combined emotional responses of the visitors. It showed how many individual reactions could become one shared interpretation of the artwork.

## 5. How did you feel about seeing and interacting with other visitors’ reflections and sketches, such as reading comments or liking sketches?

I found it interesting because it made the gallery experience feel more social and connected. Reading comments and liking sketches helped me understand that people could interpret the same artwork in very different ways.

## 6. If you were actually visiting a gallery, which feature would you be most likely to use again, and what would you change or remove?

I would probably use the reflection-sharing feature again because it gave me a reason to think more deeply about the artwork. I would improve the initial instructions and remove any unnecessary steps between tapping the NFC tag and sharing a response.
