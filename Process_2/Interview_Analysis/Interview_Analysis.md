# Gallery Echoes Interview Initial Coding

This document contains the initial codes from the Gallery Echoes prototype interviews. Similar responses from different participants are placed under the same code.

## C01 Personal Reflection and Seeing Other Visitors' Responses

**Central Idea:** Visitors see Gallery Echoes as a way to share their own responses to artworks and see how other visitors respond.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I think it’s mainly there to help you reflect on the artwork a bit, and also see what other people thought about it. It feels less like an audio guide and more like something you use to leave a quick reaction.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “I think it’s for leaving a quick reaction to an artwork and seeing what other people thought about the same one.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I think Gallery Echoes is designed to make viewing art more interactive and personal. It allows visitors to share their feelings and see how other people respond to the same artwork.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “I'd say it’s really designed to make visiting a gallery a bit more interactive and social. Like, instead of just looking at an artwork and moving on, I can actually use my phone to interact with it, record how I feel, and then see how other people responded to that exact same piece.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_3:** “To me, it’s designed to make visiting a gallery a bit more interactive and reflective. You basically tap the NFC tag next to an artwork to learn more about it, record your own thoughts or feelings, and then you get to see what other visitors noticed or shared about that exact same piece.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “Um, I think it’s basically a way to interact with the artwork a bit more. Like, normally in a gallery I would just look at something and then move to the next one. But here you can leave your reaction and also see what other people thought about it. So it feels a bit more interactive.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “I think it’s for sharing how you feel about an artwork, and then seeing how other people reacted to the same thing.”

## C02 Focus on Personal Responses

**Central Idea:** Visitors see personal feelings and observations as an important part of the experience.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “It’s more like, ‘What did you notice?’ or ‘How did this make you feel?’ rather than telling you what the artwork means.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “Yeah, more about your own response to it.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “It almost feels like a way of leaving a small trace of your own experience behind in the gallery.”

## C03 Clear Guidance and Next Steps

**Central Idea:** Visitors generally understood the experience, but some moments needed clearer instructions about what to do next.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “On P1 there was the ‘Add a thought’ button, and then P2 showed the time I’d spent with the artwork and another button saying ‘Share Feelings’. I wasn’t sure if P2 was supposed to appear automatically or if I’d done something to trigger it.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “After I tapped ‘Add a thought’, P2 came up with the time and another button. I wasn’t sure if I was meant to wait there or just keep going.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I was briefly unsure about what to do after first opening the experience. I was not immediately sure whether I should tap the artwork, scroll, or look for another instruction. A clearer prompt at the beginning would make the process easier.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “At the very beginning, I think I’d probably need a little bit of explanation about what tapping the NFC tag actually does.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_2:** “Just a little bit right after tapping the NFC tag. I wasn't entirely sure if I was supposed to leave my thoughts immediately or if I could just browse the artwork details first.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_3:** “I saw ‘Share Feelings’ and then ‘Nearby footprints’ right below it, and at first, I wasn’t entirely sure if I was supposed to share my own response first or go explore what other people had posted.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “Yeah, a little bit. Most of it was pretty clear, but at one point I wasn’t sure if I had to submit my response before I could continue.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “I think maybe the next button could be more obvious. Like, make it bigger or highlight it somehow. Even a small progress bar could help, because then I know, okay, I’m on step two or step three.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “At the beginning, yeah. When I saw the NFC tag, I knew I was probably supposed to tap it, but I wasn’t completely sure what would happen after that.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “Maybe you could have a really short instruction before that, like ‘Tap to begin’ or something. Nothing too long, just enough so people know what to expect.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “The ‘Share artwork’ button on P4 was also a bit unclear. I thought it might mean sharing it outside the app.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_3:** “The only thing I might tweak is making the difference between ‘Add a thought’ and ‘Share Feelings’ a bit more obvious, because they do sound quite similar to me.”

> **Yifan_Participant_1:** “No, I did not really feel confused during the experience. I thought the interaction was convenient and clear, especially the information panel.”

## C06 Shorter and Simpler Flow

**Central Idea:** Visitors want fewer steps and a shorter interaction process.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I think P1, P2 and P3 could probably be shorter though. It felt like a few steps before I actually got to write anything.”

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “Maybe not straight away, but I think P1 and P2 could probably be combined.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “Maybe combine P1 and P2. Then you could see the artwork info and go straight to the reflection part.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I would improve the initial instructions and remove any unnecessary steps between tapping the NFC tag and sharing a response.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “I would probably simplify some of the navigation or artwork-tracking functions if they make the overall experience too complicated.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “The only thing is, I’d want that transition to be super simple.”

> **Yifan_Participant_1:** “I would prefer the description to pop up directly on the screen instead of having to enter another page or take extra steps.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “I would keep the reflections and sketches, but maybe make the whole process slightly shorter.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “Maybe there are one or two steps that could be combined though.”

## C08 Collective Visual and Personal Contribution

**Central Idea:** Visitors understand the collective visual as many individual responses coming together, and they value seeing how their own response becomes part of it.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I thought each dot was probably another visitor’s response, and the colours showed different moods. So maybe blue means calm, yellow means curious, something like that.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “I thought each dot was another visitor’s mood or reaction. The colours probably showed different feelings.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I thought the collective visual represented the combined emotional responses of the visitors. It showed how many individual reactions could become one shared interpretation of the artwork.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “I understood it as a way of presenting visitors’ different responses or feelings about the artworks together.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “My first thought was that it represents the combined feelings or reactions of everyone who’s interacted with that piece.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_2:** “I kind of saw it as a mood board or like a living emotional heat map for the artwork.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_3:** “I figured it represented the combined reactions of all the different visitors.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “I saw it as everyone’s different reactions being put together.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “For me, it was kind of like a shared memory of the artwork.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “Everyone sees the same piece, but they leave a slightly different feeling or response. Then all of those responses come together.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “I think that was probably the most interesting part for me because you can see that your response is just one small part of something bigger.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “I like seeing how my response changes or becomes part of the final result.”

## C11 Different Views Support Reflection

**Central Idea:** Seeing other visitors’ responses can show different views, help visitors notice new details, and encourage deeper thinking.

### Original Excerpts

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “Reading comments and liking sketches helped me understand that people could interpret the same artwork in very different ways.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I would probably use the reflection-sharing feature again because it gave me a reason to think more deeply about the artwork.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “Visitors are not only looking at the artwork themselves, but can also see how other people experienced or interpreted it.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “Reading their reflections or seeing their sketches gives me a totally different perspective.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_2:** “It’s so fascinating to see what specific details catch other people's eyes.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_3:** “I really liked it because it gave me perspectives I probably wouldn’t have caught on my own.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_3:** “Seeing comments such as ‘the bridge almost disappears into the water’ or ‘I noticed the ripples before I saw the lilies’—that actually made me look at the artwork differently.”

> **Yifan_Participant_1:** “People can have different opinions about the same artwork, and seeing those different perspectives can be interesting.”

> **Yifan_Participant_1:** “Being able to see other people’s sketches could give me inspiration and help me think of new ideas.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “Sometimes another person notices something that I didn’t notice at all, so it can make me look at the artwork again.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “There were a few where I was like, ‘Oh, I didn’t think about it that way.’”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “So that could actually make me look back at the artwork and notice something different.”

## C12 Casual and Anonymous Sharing Reduces Pressure

**Central Idea:** Casual and anonymous sharing can make visitors feel more comfortable contributing their own responses or sketches.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I also liked that the sketches looked pretty casual. They didn’t look like professional drawings or anything, so it made it feel less intimidating.”

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I think if names and profiles were attached, I’d probably think about it more before posting. Anonymous feels easier.”

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “Especially if I could see that other people were just doing quick sketches too. I wouldn’t feel like it had to look good.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “If other people are just doing quick sketches, I wouldn’t feel like mine had to look good.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_2:** “I did feel a tiny bit shy about posting my own sketch, to be honest.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_2:** “I might want a clearer option to post completely anonymously.”

---

## C13 Social Features Should Stay Simple

**Central Idea:** Visitors like social interaction, but do not want the experience to feel like a social media platform.

### Original Excerpts

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “I am a little less sure about the ‘liking’ feature, though, because it starts to feel a bit too much like social media.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “I wouldn’t want people just making sketches because they’re chasing likes, you know?”

> **Mingjie_Interview_3:** “I do think the ‘likes’ should stay fairly subtle, though, just so it doesn’t start feeling too much like a typical social media feed.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “But I probably wouldn’t want too many social features. Like, if there are followers, profiles, lots of comments and things like that, then it starts feeling a bit too much like social media.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “I think keeping it simple works better for a gallery.”

---

## C14 Digital Content Should Stay Lightweight

**Central Idea:** Digital interaction and social content should stay short and limited so they do not distract visitors from the physical artwork.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I think it would distract me a bit. I’d rather look at the actual artwork than spend too much time doing things on my phone.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “If I’m already standing in front of the artwork, I’d rather look at that than spend more time on my phone.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “I really wouldn't want to spend too much time staring at my phone while I'm standing right in front of the actual physical artwork.”

> **Mingjie_Interview_1:** “I’d try to make the phone interaction as short as possible, so the tech actually supports looking at the artwork rather than just distracting me from it.”

> **Yifan_Participant_2:** “I think if it takes too long, people might just skip it, especially when they’re walking around the gallery.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “In a gallery I probably wouldn’t want to spend too long on my phone or on a screen, because I still want to look at the actual artwork.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “If there are, like, hundreds of comments on the screen, I probably wouldn’t read them. It could get distracting quite quickly.”

> **Yifan_Participant_3:** “Maybe showing a few responses first and letting people choose to see more would work better.”
