# Figma Prototype Test Plan

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this test is to evaluate the first Figma prototype of the **Ambient Footprint** concept before developing the functional coded prototype.

The evaluation focuses on:

* usability and discoverability;
* concept and content comprehension;
* social and experiential value;
* user needs, preferences, and social boundaries.

The findings will be used to decide which features should be retained, revised, removed, or prioritised in the next prototype.

---

# 2. Evaluation Question 1 — Usability and Discoverability

**Can users successfully navigate the prototype and complete its core interactions without unnecessary assistance?**

## What will be tested

* Whether users can recognise available interactions.
* Whether users can understand what actions are possible.
* Whether users can complete the main interaction flow.
* Whether users can navigate without repeated errors or researcher guidance.
* Whether important functions are easy to discover.

## Method

**Task-based usability testing + observation + think-aloud**

## Why this method

Task-based testing shows whether users can actually complete the intended interactions rather than only reporting that the interface feels easy to use.

Observation and think-aloud help identify where users become confused and what causes the problem.

## Data collected

* Task completion
* Assistance required
* Interaction errors
* Hesitation
* Incorrect navigation
* Think-aloud comments

## Success criteria

The interaction is considered successful when most participants can complete the core tasks independently and no repeated major usability issue prevents them from progressing.

Repeated confusion, failure, or researcher assistance indicates that the interaction should be revised.

---

# 3. Evaluation Question 2 — Concept and Content Comprehension

**Do users correctly understand the meaning and purpose of Ambient Footprints and the information presented by the prototype?**

## What will be tested

* Whether users understand what an Ambient Footprint represents.
* Whether they understand where it comes from.
* Whether they understand its relationship with an artwork or location.
* Whether they understand what happens when they leave their own footprint.
* Whether interface information and visual cues are clear.

## Method

**Structured comprehension questions + clarity rating**

## Why this method

Because Ambient Footprint is an unfamiliar concept, users may be able to operate the interface without correctly understanding its meaning.

Asking users to explain the concept in their own words provides stronger evidence of actual comprehension, while the rating provides a simple measure of perceived clarity.

## Data collected

* Correct understanding
* Partial understanding
* Incorrect understanding
* Clarity rating
* Recurring misunderstandings

## Success criteria

The concept is considered sufficiently understandable when most participants can correctly explain its basic purpose and no consistent misunderstanding appears across participants.

If users can operate the interface but misunderstand the concept, the communication and representation of the feature should be revised.

---

# 4. Evaluation Question 3 — Social and Experiential Value

**Do Ambient Footprints add meaningful social and experiential value without unnecessarily interrupting the gallery experience?**

## What will be tested

* Perceived connection with other visitors.
* Whether footprints add value to artwork interpretation.
* Whether the interaction encourages curiosity or reflection.
* Whether the digital layer complements the gallery experience.
* Whether the interaction feels natural.
* Whether the system feels distracting or intrusive.
* Whether users would want to use the system in a real gallery.

## Method

**Post-task rating scale + qualitative interview**

## Why this method

Rating scales are suitable for comparing perceived value, connection, and distraction across participants.

Interviews are used because these experiences are subjective and the reasons behind positive or negative ratings are important for deciding how the design should change.

## Data collected

* Social connection rating
* Experiential value rating
* Naturalness rating
* Distraction rating
* Willingness to use
* Participant explanations and comments

## Success criteria

The concept is considered promising when participants generally perceive social or experiential value and distraction does not emerge as a repeated major concern.

If users can use the feature but repeatedly describe it as unnecessary or intrusive, the feature itself should be reconsidered.

---

# 5. Evaluation Question 4 — User Needs, Preferences and Social Boundaries

**What types of footprints, interactions, and levels of system presence do users prefer or reject?**

## What will be tested

* Preferred footprint content.
* Content users are willing to contribute.
* Preferred interaction timing and frequency.
* Situations in which users prefer no interaction.
* Privacy expectations.
* Anonymity preferences.
* Sources of discomfort.
* Willingness to contribute.
* Preferred and unwanted features.
* Priorities for the next prototype.

## Method

**Post-test interview + feature ranking**

## Why this method

Interviews are suitable because users may raise needs, concerns, or social boundaries that cannot be predicted in advance.

Feature ranking helps turn individual preferences into clearer priorities for the next prototype iteration.

## Data collected

* Preferred content
* Unwanted content
* Privacy concerns
* Preferred interaction timing
* Preferred level of anonymity
* Willingness to contribute
* Unwanted interruptions
* Feature priorities

## Success criteria

This question does not require a strict pass or fail result.

Its success is determined by whether the findings provide clear evidence for feature prioritisation and design decisions.

Repeatedly valued features should be prioritised, while features associated with repeated discomfort, low value, or unwanted interruption should be revised or removed.

---

# 6. Participants

A small group of participants will be recruited for this formative evaluation.

Participants should preferably have some previous experience with galleries, museums, exhibitions, or similar cultural spaces.

Variation in gallery experience is useful for identifying whether the concept is understandable and valuable to different types of visitors.

The aim is not statistical generalisation, but to identify recurring design problems and user needs before further implementation.

---

# 7. Testing Procedure

The test will follow this sequence:

1. Introduction and consent.
2. Short participant background questions.
3. Task-based interaction with the Figma prototype.
4. Observation and think-aloud.
5. Concept comprehension check.
6. Short quantitative evaluation.
7. Post-test interview.
8. Feature ranking.

Participants will interact with the prototype before receiving detailed explanations so that their natural understanding and behaviour can be observed.

---

# 8. Data Analysis

## Quantitative Data

Quantitative data will be used to identify overall patterns in:

* task completion;
* independent interaction;
* comprehension;
* clarity;
* social connection;
* experiential value;
* distraction;
* willingness to use or contribute;
* feature preference.

The purpose is descriptive rather than statistically generalisable.

## Qualitative Data

Observation notes, think-aloud comments, and interview responses will be grouped into recurring themes, including:

* usability problems;
* misunderstandings;
* social value;
* distraction;
* privacy concerns;
* unwanted interaction;
* preferred content;
* contribution motivation;
* preferred level of system presence.

Qualitative findings will mainly be used to explain why particular patterns appear and to identify issues that structured ratings cannot capture.

---

# 9. How the Findings Inform Iteration

The test findings will be used to distinguish between different types of design problems.

* If a feature is valued but difficult to use, the interaction should be redesigned.
* If a feature is easy to use but provides little perceived value, its purpose should be reconsidered.
* If the concept is misunderstood, its representation and communication should be improved.
* If the system feels distracting or intrusive, its frequency, visibility, or level of presence should be reduced.
* If the same unmet need appears repeatedly, it may become a new design requirement.

This ensures that the next prototype iteration is based on collected evidence rather than designer preference alone.

---

# 10. Expected Outcome

The test is expected to provide three types of evidence:

**Interaction Evidence**
Whether users can understand and operate the prototype.

**Concept Evidence**
Whether Ambient Footprints are understandable and provide meaningful social and experiential value.

**Design Direction Evidence**
Which features and interaction approaches should be retained, revised, removed, or prioritised in the next prototype.

The purpose of this evaluation is to reduce uncertainty around the key design decisions before developing the functional prototype.
