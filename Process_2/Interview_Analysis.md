# Gallery Echoes Interview Initial Coding

This document contains the initial codes from the Gallery Echoes prototype interviews. Similar responses from different participants are placed under the same code.

## C01 Personal Reflection and Seeing Other Visitors' Responses

**Central Idea:** Visitors see Gallery Echoes as a way to reflect on artworks and see how other people respond.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I think it’s mainly there to help you reflect on the artwork a bit, and also see what other people thought about it. It feels less like an audio guide and more like something you use to leave a quick reaction.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “I think it’s for leaving a quick reaction to an artwork and seeing what other people thought about the same one.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I think Gallery Echoes is designed to make viewing art more interactive and personal. It allows visitors to share their feelings and see how other people respond to the same artwork.”

---

## C02 Focus on Personal Responses

**Central Idea:** Visitors think the experience focuses on their own feelings and responses to artworks.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “It’s more like, ‘What did you notice?’ or ‘How did this make you feel?’ rather than telling you what the artwork means.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “Yeah, more about your own response to it.”

---

## C03 Unclear Flow Between Steps

**Central Idea:** Visitors were sometimes unsure about what to do next during the interaction.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “On P1 there was the ‘Add a thought’ button, and then P2 showed the time I’d spent with the artwork and another button saying ‘Share Feelings’. I wasn’t sure if P2 was supposed to appear automatically or if I’d done something to trigger it.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “After I tapped ‘Add a thought’, P2 came up with the time and another button. I wasn’t sure if I was meant to wait there or just keep going.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I was briefly unsure about what to do after first opening the experience. I was not immediately sure whether I should tap the artwork, scroll, or look for another instruction.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “Sometimes I could not open certain features or pages, so I could not really see how the interaction between different pages was supposed to work. This made some parts of the experience a little confusing.”

---

## C04 Unclear “Share Artwork” Button

**Central Idea:** The “Share artwork” button could be misunderstood as sharing outside the app.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “And on P4, the button saying ‘Share artwork’ confused me for a second. I thought it might open the normal phone sharing menu, like sending it to someone, rather than sharing my response in the app.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “The ‘Share artwork’ button on P4 was also a bit unclear. I thought it might mean sharing it outside the app.”

---

## C05 Small Confusion Did Not Stop the Interaction

**Central Idea:** Visitors sometimes paused when the interface was unclear, but they were still able to continue.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “No, I still tapped it. I just paused for a second because I wasn’t completely sure what would happen.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “Not much. I still tapped it, but I hesitated for a second.”

---

## C06 Overall Interaction Flow Is Easy to Understand

**Central Idea:** Visitors generally understood the main steps from viewing an artwork to leaving a response.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “Mostly, yeah. I understood the overall flow. You tap the tag, look at the artwork, choose how you feel, and then leave a response.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “Mostly, yeah. I understood what I was supposed to do, but I think there were a few too many screens before I could actually leave a response.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “Yes, the process mostly made sense. I could view the artwork first and then share my feelings, so the main steps felt logical.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “After tapping the NFC tag, I could access the information and interaction related to that specific artwork, and the process of viewing the artwork and then sharing my feelings made sense to me.”

---

## C07 Too Many Steps Before Leaving a Response

**Central Idea:** Some visitors felt that the interaction could be shorter and have fewer steps.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I think P1, P2 and P3 could probably be shorter though. It felt like a few steps before I actually got to write anything.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “I understood what I was supposed to do, but I think there were a few too many screens before I could actually leave a response.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I would improve the initial instructions and remove any unnecessary steps between tapping the NFC tag and sharing a response.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “I would probably simplify some of the navigation or artwork-tracking functions if they make the overall experience too complicated.”

---

## C08 P1 and P2 Could Be Combined

**Central Idea:** Visitors suggested combining P1 and P2 to make the interaction shorter and simpler.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “Maybe not straight away, but I think P1 and P2 could probably be combined. Like, show the artwork name, how long I’ve been there, and the prompt on the same page.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “Maybe combine P1 and P2. Then you could see the artwork info and go straight to the reflection part.”

---

## C09 Mood Tags Make Responding Easier

**Central Idea:** Mood tags provide an easy way to respond without writing much.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I liked P3 because the question ‘What caught your eye in this artwork?’ is pretty easy to answer. And the mood tags help if you don’t really want to type much.”

---

## C10 Collective Visual Shows Shared Responses

**Central Idea:** Visitors understand the collective visual as a combination of different visitors’ feelings and responses.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “I thought each dot was probably another visitor’s response, and the colours showed different moods. So maybe blue means calm, yellow means curious, something like that.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “I thought each dot was another visitor’s mood or reaction. The colours probably showed different feelings.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I thought the collective visual represented the combined emotional responses of the visitors. It showed how many individual reactions could become one shared interpretation of the artwork.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “I understood it as a way of presenting visitors’ different responses or feelings about the artworks together.”

---

## C11 Seeing Other Responses Creates Social Connection

**Central Idea:** Seeing other visitors’ responses can make the gallery experience feel more social and connected.

### Original Excerpts

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “When my dot appeared, it was kind of nice because you could see that other people had also reacted to the same artwork.”

> **Elaris_Interview_1:** “You don’t really talk to strangers in galleries, so it was interesting to see that other people had felt something too.”

> **Elaris_Interview_2:** “You can see that other people reacted to the same artwork, even if you don’t talk to them.”

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I found it interesting because it made the gallery experience feel more social and connected.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “I especially liked that this could connect people who are interested in or appreciate the same artwork.”

---

## C12 Other Visitors' Responses Show Different Views

**Central Idea:** Visitors value seeing how other people experience and interpret the same artwork.

### Original Excerpts

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “Reading comments and liking sketches helped me understand that people could interpret the same artwork in very different ways.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “Visitors are not only looking at the artwork themselves, but can also see how other people experienced or interpreted it.”

---

## C13 Reflection Can Support Deeper Thinking

**Central Idea:** Sharing a reflection can encourage visitors to think more deeply about an artwork.

### Original Excerpts

> **Fu_Interview_1:** “I would probably use the reflection-sharing feature again because it gave me a reason to think more deeply about the artwork.”

---

## C14 Social Features Can Create Common Topics

**Central Idea:** Shared responses can give visitors with similar interests something to talk about.

### Original Excerpts

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “It gives visitors a common topic to talk about and makes the experience feel more interactive rather than just viewing the artwork individually.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “It gives people with similar interests something to talk about and could make the gallery experience more meaningful and engaging.”

---

## C15 Incomplete Prototype Makes Some Features Hard to Understand

**Central Idea:** Some visitors found it difficult to judge parts of the experience because the prototype was not fully developed.

### Original Excerpts

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “Sometimes I could not open certain features or pages, so I could not really see how the interaction between different pages was supposed to work.”

> **Fu_Interview_2:** “However, because the prototype is not completely developed yet, I do not think this feature was presented very clearly or completely. It was therefore a little difficult to fully understand what the final version would look like.”
