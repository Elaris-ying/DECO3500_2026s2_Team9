# Interview 2

## 1. Can you briefly describe what you think Gallery Echoes is for, in your own words?

I think Gallery Echoes is basically a practical gallery or museum navigation app. It helps visitors explore artworks in the gallery, find where different artworks are located, and keep track of what they have already seen. It also allows visitors to interact with the artworks and record their experience while visiting the gallery.

## 2. Was there any point during the experience where you were unsure what to do next? What happened?

Yes. There were a few moments when I was not sure what to do next, mainly because the prototype is not fully completed yet. Sometimes I could not open certain features or pages, so I could not really see how the interaction between different pages was supposed to work. This made some parts of the experience a little confusing.

## 3. After tapping the NFC tag, did the steps from viewing the artwork to sharing your feelings make sense to you? Why or why not?

Yes, I think this feature was designed quite well. After tapping the NFC tag, I could access the information and interaction related to that specific artwork, and the process of viewing the artwork and then sharing my feelings made sense to me.

I especially liked that this could connect people who are interested in or appreciate the same artwork. It gives visitors a common topic to talk about and makes the experience feel more interactive rather than just viewing the artwork individually.

## 4. What did you think the collective visual represented?

I think the idea of the collective visual was quite good. I understood it as a way of presenting visitors’ different responses or feelings about the artworks together.

However, because the prototype is not completely developed yet, I do not think this feature was presented very clearly or completely. It was therefore a little difficult to fully understand what the final version would look like.

## 5. How did you feel about seeing and interacting with other visitors’ reflections and sketches, such as reading comments or liking sketches?

I actually liked this feature. I think being able to see other visitors’ reflections, sketches, and comments makes the gallery experience more interactive. Visitors are not only looking at the artwork themselves, but can also see how other people experienced or interpreted it.

I think this social aspect is one of the stronger features of the prototype.

## 6. If you were actually visiting a gallery, which feature would you be most likely to use again, and what would you change or remove?

I would probably use the social and community features the most, especially reading other visitors’ reflections and sharing my own thoughts.

For me, after visiting a gallery, I probably would not care that much about remembering exactly where every artwork was located. However, being able to communicate with other people who have seen and appreciated the same artwork would be much more interesting. It gives people with similar interests something to talk about and could make the gallery experience more meaningful and engaging.

I would probably simplify some of the navigation or artwork-tracking functions if they make the overall experience too complicated.
