Interviewer: Can you briefly describe what you think Gallery Echoes is for, in your own words?

Participant: I'd say it’s really designed to make visiting a gallery a bit more interactive and social. Like, instead of just looking at an artwork and moving on, I can actually use my phone to interact with it, record how I feel, and then see how other people responded to that exact same piece. It almost feels like a way of leaving a small trace of your own experience behind in the gallery.

Interviewer: Was there any point during the experience where you were unsure what to do next? What happened?

Participant: Hmm, at the very beginning, I think I’d probably need a little bit of explanation about what tapping the NFC tag actually does. Once I got into the experience, the general flow made sense, but I could see myself hesitating a bit when moving between just looking at the artwork and actually contributing my own response. I think I’d want a clearer indication of what exactly I’m expected to do, and maybe some reassurance that contributing is totally optional.

Interviewer: After tapping the NFC tag, did the steps from viewing the artwork to sharing your feelings make sense to you? Why or why not?

Participant: Yeah, mostly! I really like that I get to encounter the artwork first and then respond to it. It gives me some time to form my own impression before I start seeing or thinking about what other people said. Sharing a feeling afterwards just feels like a natural next step. The only thing is, I’d want that transition to be super simple. I really wouldn't want to spend too much time staring at my phone while I'm standing right in front of the actual physical artwork.

Interviewer: What did you think the collective visual represented?

Participant: My first thought was that it represents the combined feelings or reactions of everyone who’s interacted with that piece. So, rather than just showing one person’s opinion, it becomes this kind of emotional picture of the whole audience. I think that’s super interesting! But I definitely would want to understand what the different parts of the visual actually mean, and exactly how my specific response contributes to the whole thing.

Interviewer: How did you feel about seeing and interacting with other visitors’ reflections and sketches, such as reading comments or liking sketches?

Participant: I actually found that part really interesting. Normally, when I’m in a gallery, I see other people looking at an artwork, but I have no idea what they’re actually thinking. Reading their reflections or seeing their sketches gives me a totally different perspective. Honestly, I’d probably read a lot more than I’d post myself. I am a little less sure about the "liking" feature, though, because it starts to feel a bit too much like social media. I wouldn’t want people just making sketches because they’re chasing likes, you know?

Interviewer: If you were actually visiting a gallery, which feature would you be most likely to use again, and what would you change or remove?

Participant: I’d definitely use the collective response and the other visitors’ reflections again. Those features give me something extra that I just can’t get from a standard gallery label. I also really like the NFC idea—tapping something is just so much quicker than downloading a whole app and searching for a specific artwork. As for changes, I’d probably simplify the social features, especially the likes. I'd keep the focus more on sharing and discovering different interpretations. Oh, and I’d try to make the phone interaction as short as possible, so the tech actually supports looking at the artwork rather than just distracting me from it.
