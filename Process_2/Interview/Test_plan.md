# Prototype Evaluation Plan

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this evaluation was to gather user feedback on the
first prototype of Gallery Echoes before further developing the
functional prototype.

The evaluation focused on four areas:

- understanding of the overall concept;
- clarity of the core interaction flow;
- perceived social and experiential value;
- feature preferences and directions for refinement.

The evaluation was exploratory and formative rather than intended
to produce statistically generalisable results.

---

## 2. Method

A semi-structured interview was conducted after participants
experienced the prototype.

The interview format was chosen because the current prototype was
still incomplete and the main purpose at this stage was to understand
participants' interpretations, expectations, preferences and concerns.

Rather than measuring usability through formal HCI metrics, the
evaluation focused on participants' descriptions of their experience.

Participants were encouraged to explain:

- what they believed the system was for;
- which interactions were clear or unclear;
- how they interpreted the social features;
- whether they valued seeing other visitors' responses;
- which features they would use, change or remove.

---

## 3. Evaluation Questions

### EQ1 — Concept Comprehension

Do participants understand the purpose of Gallery Echoes and the
meaning of its main features?

### EQ2 — Interaction Clarity

Do participants understand the core interaction flow, particularly
the transition from encountering an artwork to leaving and viewing
visitor responses?

### EQ3 — Social and Experiential Value

Do participants perceive value in anonymously encountering and
sharing visitor perspectives?

### EQ4 — Feature Priorities

Which aspects of the prototype do participants value, reject, or
suggest changing?

---

## 4. Participants

Participants were recruited as proxy users for this formative
evaluation.

Preference was given to people with some prior experience visiting
galleries, museums or exhibitions.

The aim was not statistical generalisation, but to identify recurring
responses that could inform the next iteration.

---

## 5. Procedure

1. Introduce the prototype and evaluation activity.
2. Allow the participant to experience the prototype.
3. Conduct a semi-structured interview.
4. Ask follow-up questions where clarification was required.
5. Record participant responses for later comparison.

---

## 6. Interview Questions

1. Can you briefly describe what you think Gallery Echoes is for,
   in your own words?

2. Was there any point during the experience where you were unsure
   what to do next? What happened?

3. After tapping the NFC tag, did the steps from viewing the artwork
   to sharing your feelings make sense to you? Why or why not?

4. What did you think the collective visual represented?

5. How did you feel about seeing and interacting with other visitors'
   reflections and sketches?

6. If you were actually visiting a gallery, which feature would you
   be most likely to use again, and what would you change or remove?

---

## 7. Analysis

Interview responses were compared across participants to identify
recurring responses and contrasting perspectives.

The analysis focused on four areas:

- concept understanding;
- interaction clarity;
- perceived social value;
- feature priorities and concerns.

Repeated responses were treated as stronger indications for design
refinement, while individual comments were retained where they
revealed a potentially important issue or alternative perspective.

The findings were used to inform decisions about what should be
retained, revised, simplified or further developed in the next
prototype.
