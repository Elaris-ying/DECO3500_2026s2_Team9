Interviewer: Can you briefly describe what you think Gallery Echoes is for, in your own words?

Participant: To me, it feels like a way to break the silence of a traditional gallery. Instead of just walking around in your own little bubble, it connects you with other visitors. It basically turns looking at art from a solo activity into this shared community experience where you can actually see what everyone else is feeling.

Interviewer: Was there any point during the experience where you were unsure what to do next? What happened?

Participant: Just a little bit right after tapping the NFC tag. I wasn't entirely sure if I was supposed to leave my thoughts immediately or if I could just browse the artwork details first. I think I just needed a quick on-screen prompt saying something like, "Take your time to look, then share when you're ready."

Interviewer: After tapping the NFC tag, did the steps from viewing the artwork to sharing your feelings make sense to you? Why or why not?

Participant: The general flow definitely makes sense. Though, honestly, I found myself wanting to peek at what a few other people wrote before sharing my own feelings. Sometimes it’s a little intimidating to just put your thoughts out there from scratch! But physically going from viewing the art on the wall to interacting on the phone felt pretty smooth overall.

Interviewer: What did you think the collective visual represented?

Participant: I kind of saw it as a mood board or like a living emotional heat map for the artwork. It’s a really beautiful way to visualize the room's energy. I did spend a little time trying to figure out which specific color or shape was generated by my own input, though! I'd love to know exactly how my piece fits into the puzzle.

Interviewer: How did you feel about seeing and interacting with other visitors’ reflections and sketches, such as reading comments or liking sketches?

Participant: I absolutely loved the sketches part! It’s so fascinating to see what specific details catch other people's eyes. As for the "likes," I actually didn't mind them at all—it’s a nice, quick way to show appreciation for someone's thought without having to type out a whole reply. I did feel a tiny bit shy about posting my own sketch, to be honest.

Interviewer: If you were actually visiting a gallery, which feature would you be most likely to use again, and what would you change or remove?

Participant: I’d definitely use the feature to read other people's reflections and look at their sketches again. That added so much value to the visit. If I were to change something, I might want a clearer option to post completely anonymously. Sometimes you want to share a really deep feeling about a piece but don't necessarily want your profile or name attached to it.
