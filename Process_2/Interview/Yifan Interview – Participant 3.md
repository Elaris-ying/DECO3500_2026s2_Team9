# User Testing Interview – Participant 3

### 1. What do you think Gallery Echoes is for?

**Participant:**
I think it’s for sharing how you feel about an artwork, and then seeing how other people reacted to the same thing.

It feels a bit like you’re leaving something behind after you see the artwork, instead of just looking at it and walking away.

### 2. Was there any point where you were unsure what to do next?

**Participant:**
At the beginning, yeah. When I saw the NFC tag, I knew I was probably supposed to tap it, but I wasn’t completely sure what would happen after that.

Once the next screen came up, it was fine. Maybe you could have a really short instruction before that, like “Tap to begin” or something. Nothing too long, just enough so people know what to expect.

### 3. Did the NFC interaction and overall process make sense?

**Participant:**
Yeah, mostly.

I think looking at the artwork first, then adding a short response, and then seeing it become part of the visual makes sense.

Maybe there are one or two steps that could be combined though. In a gallery I probably wouldn’t want to spend too long on my phone or on a screen, because I still want to look at the actual artwork.

### 4. What did the collective visual represent to you?

**Participant:**
For me, it was kind of like a shared memory of the artwork.

Everyone sees the same piece, but they leave a slightly different feeling or response. Then all of those responses come together.

I think that was probably the most interesting part for me because you can see that your response is just one small part of something bigger.

### 5. How did you feel about interacting with other visitors’ reflections and sketches?

**Participant:**
I liked reading some of them. There were a few where I was like, “Oh, I didn’t think about it that way.”

So that could actually make me look back at the artwork and notice something different.

But I think there needs to be a limit. If there are, like, hundreds of comments on the screen, I probably wouldn’t read them. It could get distracting quite quickly.

Maybe showing a few responses first and letting people choose to see more would work better.

### 6. Which feature would you use again, and what would you change?

**Participant:**
Probably leaving a short reflection and then seeing the collective visual. I like seeing how my response changes or becomes part of the final result.

I’d keep it quite simple though. Maybe fewer steps, and I think looking at other people’s comments should be optional.

Sometimes I just want to have my own reaction to the artwork first. I don’t always want to read what everyone else thinks before I decide how I feel.
