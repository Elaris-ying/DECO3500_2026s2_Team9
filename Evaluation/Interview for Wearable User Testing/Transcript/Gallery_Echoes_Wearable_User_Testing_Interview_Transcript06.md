# Participant 1 — Interview Record

## 1. First Impression

### Q: What do you think this wearable device is designed to do?

**Participant Response:**  
I think it is used to change colours to express how you feel.

### Q: Was its purpose clear to you at first?

**Participant Response:**  
Not really. I did not fully understand it at first, but it became clearer after you explained it to me.

### Q: Was there anything that confused you?

**Participant Response:**  
I was wondering why the colour can only be changed on the phone instead of directly on the wearable device itself.

### Q: Without explanation, would you know what to do next?

**Participant Response:**  
Probably not. I would not be very sure what to do next. Maybe it could be connected with an app that gives some guidance.

---

## 2. Wearability

### Q: How did the wearable device feel when you were wearing it?

**Participant Response:**  
It felt fine. I did not really notice it that much while wearing it.

### Q: Did it feel comfortable?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, it was fairly comfortable.

### Q: Did it feel too large, heavy, or distracting?

**Participant Response:**  
No. The weight was not noticeable.

### Q: Would you feel comfortable wearing it during a gallery visit?

**Participant Response:**  
I do not think it necessarily has to be worn on the wrist. It could also be designed as a pendant or a tag that can be attached somewhere else.

### Q: Would you prefer the device to be more visible or more subtle?

**Participant Response:**  
I would prefer it to be more subtle. The light should also be less bright so that it does not disturb other people in the gallery.

---

## 3. Palm Interaction

### Q: How natural did it feel to interact with the interface on your palm?

**Participant Response:**  
It was okay, but I still think using a phone is more convenient.

### Q: Was it easy to understand how to choose a colour and view an Echo?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, it was easy to understand.

### Q: Which part felt the most natural?

**Participant Response:**  
Changing the colour felt very smooth.

### Q: Which part felt awkward or unclear?

**Participant Response:**  
I think there should be a button that allows me to turn the light off.

### Q: Would you prefer this interaction or using a phone? Why?

**Participant Response:**  
I would prefer using a phone because phones are already very convenient and I would not need to learn how to use a new device.

---

## 4. Attention to the Artwork

### Q: Did the wearable help you stay focused on the artwork, or did it distract you?

**Participant Response:**  
It was a little distracting, but it was also fun. When I visit a gallery, I am not always fully focused on every artwork anyway, especially when I do not like a painting or do not understand it.

### Q: Which part of the interaction distracted you the most?

**Participant Response:**  
No specific response was recorded for this question.

### Q: Did the vibration feel helpful or annoying?

**Participant Response:**  
I think it would be helpful. For example, it would be useful if the device vibrated when an artwork was automatically saved to my collection, so I would know that it had been recorded.

### Q: Did the “Look Again” prompt make you look at the artwork differently?

**Participant Response:**  
No. I do not really like going back to something I have already seen.

### Q: How long do you think each interaction should take?

**Participant Response:**  
Around three to five seconds.

---

## 5. Echoes and Social Awareness

### Q: How did seeing other visitors’ Echoes affect your experience?

**Participant Response:**  
If someone left the same colour as me, I would feel like that person has a similar taste to mine. I think that would make me a little happy.

### Q: Did the Echoes help you notice something new about the artwork?

**Participant Response:**  
I am not really sure.

### Q: Would you want to see other visitors’ Echoes? Why or why not?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes. I would like to know whether other people have similar tastes to me.

### Q: Did other people’s Echoes influence your own interpretation?

**Participant Response:**  
No.

### Q: Does this feel less stressful than talking directly to strangers?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, it feels less stressful.

---

## 6. Choice and Control

### Q: Did you feel that you had enough control while using the device?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes.

### Q: Would you like to be able to skip or ignore an Echo?

**Participant Response:**  
Either way is fine for me.

### Q: Is it important for you to be able to turn off or ignore the vibration?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, that is important. If I move through the gallery quite quickly, I would not want the device to keep vibrating all the time.

### Q: Would you prefer the system to give suggestions automatically, or would you rather choose when to interact?

**Participant Response:**  
I would rather decide for myself when to interact. If I forget about the feature, that probably means I do not need it at that moment.

### Q: Did any part of the experience feel forced?

**Participant Response:**  
No.

---

## 7. Overall Experience

### Q: If this device were available in a real gallery, would you use it? Why or why not?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, I would. I sometimes forget the things I have seen, so doing small interactions during the visit could help strengthen my memory of the artworks.

### Q: What was the most useful or interesting feature?

**Participant Response:**  
Probably using colour to express how I feel about an artwork.

### Q: Was there any feature you felt was unnecessary?

**Participant Response:**  
Uploading sketches or copies of artworks felt unnecessary to me. I do not think many people would make sketches in the first place, and even fewer people would want to share them.

### Q: What would you change about the experience?

**Participant Response:**  
No specific response was recorded for this question.

### Q: If you could improve only one thing, what would it be?

**Participant Response:**  
I would change the form of the wearable. It could become something like a pendant or a tag instead of a wristband. I would also want a button that lets me turn the light off.
