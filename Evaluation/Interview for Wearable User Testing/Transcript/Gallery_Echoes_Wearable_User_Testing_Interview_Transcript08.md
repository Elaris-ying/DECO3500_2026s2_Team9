# Gallery Echoes — Wearable User Testing Interview Transcript 08

## Introduction

**Interviewer:** Hey, thanks for staying a few minutes after the prototype test. I'm going to ask about what you found easy, confusing, or just not that useful. Before we begin, are you comfortable with me recording your answers for our design notes?

**Participant:** Yep, that's okay.

**Interviewer:** Cool. Just so I have a bit of context, do you visit art galleries often?

**Participant:** Not really. Maybe if a friend wants to go, or if there's an exhibition everyone's talking about. I don't know much about art, to be honest.

## Interview

**Interviewer:** Did that affect how you approached this prototype?

**Participant:** Probably. I actually liked that it asked me for a feeling instead of, like, what I thought the artist was trying to say. I can answer how something feels. I can't always explain why it's important.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about choosing your first colour.

**Participant:** I picked blue, but then I thought, wait, am I saying I'm sad? Because I wasn't sad. I just thought the painting felt calm. So I hesitated and nearly changed it.

**Interviewer:** What made you decide to keep it?

**Participant:** I figured it was meant to be personal. But maybe the first time you use it, there could be a really short example so you know there isn't a correct meaning.

**Interviewer:** How did you find using your palm rather than a phone screen?

**Participant:** It was kind of fun, actually. A bit futuristic. [laughs] I messed up the hand position the first time, then it was fine. I liked that I didn't need to unlock my phone and search for something.

**Interviewer:** Was anything unclear after you made a selection?

**Participant:** Yes. I thought I had pressed it, but I wasn't sure if it had saved. I was waiting for something else to happen. Then I saw the next part and realised, okay, we're moving on.

**Interviewer:** Did you pay attention to the painting while using it?

**Participant:** Mostly. The first time, no, because I was concentrating on my hand. The second time I looked at the artwork first and then did the colour thing, so that was better. I think people need one practice go.

**Interviewer:** You also tried viewing other people's Echoes. What was your reaction to that?

**Participant:** That was my favourite bit. One of the colours was completely different from mine and I was like, huh, what are they seeing that I'm not? It's less intimidating than asking a stranger what they think.

**Interviewer:** Would you want to know who left each Echo?

**Participant:** No, I don't think so. Actually, I'd prefer not to. If it had profiles and comments, I'd probably stop paying attention to the painting and start reading those instead.

**Interviewer:** What happened when you got the "Look Again" prompt?

**Participant:** I looked at the corner of the painting, which I hadn't really noticed. So that worked for me. But I wouldn't want the prompt after every single action. It would get repetitive.

**Interviewer:** How did the wearable feel on your wrist?

**Participant:** It was okay. Maybe a little bulky, but I forgot about it when I wasn't interacting. I'm more worried about looking a bit strange holding my hand up in a public space. Here, everyone was doing the test, so it felt normal.

**Interviewer:** Is there a situation where you'd choose not to use it?

**Participant:** If the gallery was really crowded, or if I was carrying a drink or a bag. I'd probably just keep walking. It should be easy to skip without feeling like you've broken something.

**Interviewer:** Suppose you could change only one part. What would you choose?

**Participant:** Hmm. Maybe the first-use instructions. Not a big tutorial, just one quick example of an Echo and what you do with the colours. Then I'd feel a lot more confident.

## Closing

**Interviewer:** And overall, would you try something like this during a gallery visit?

**Participant:** Yeah, I would. Maybe not at every painting, but it made it easier to have an opinion without needing to know lots about art. That was nice.

**Interviewer:** Great, thanks. Anything else before we finish?

**Participant:** No, I think that's it.

**Interviewer:** Thanks again for your time.

**Participant:** All good.
