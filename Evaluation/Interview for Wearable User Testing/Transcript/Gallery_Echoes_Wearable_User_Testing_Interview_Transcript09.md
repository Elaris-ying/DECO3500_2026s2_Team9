# Gallery Echoes — Wearable User Testing Interview Transcript 06

## Introduction

**Interviewer:** Hi, thanks for trying out the wristband. Before we start, is it okay if I record our conversation? It'll just help us remember your feedback. You can skip anything you don't want to answer.

**Participant:** Yeah, that's fine.

**Interviewer:** Great. I'm interested in what actually happened for you, including anything that was awkward. There's no right way to use it. What was going through your mind when you first put it on?

**Participant:** Um, I thought it was going to give me information about the painting, like an audio guide but on my wrist. So the emotion thing was a bit of a surprise.

## Interview

**Interviewer:** What made you realise it wasn't quite that kind of guide?

**Participant:** When I saw the colours. I was like, oh, I'm supposed to respond to the artwork, not get facts about it. I didn't really understand that until I tried it.

**Interviewer:** Can you talk me through the first interaction?

**Participant:** The wristband vibrated and I looked at my wrist first. Then I remembered I had to open my hand. I kind of held it there for a second because I wasn't sure if I should tap straight away or wait.

**Interviewer:** And once you made a selection?

**Participant:** That part was quick. I picked a colour almost immediately. I just wasn't totally sure it had gone through. I looked back at my hand, like, did I actually send that?

**Interviewer:** What would have helped you know?

**Participant:** Maybe just a tiny confirmation. Not a whole message. A brief pulse or something, so you don't need to keep checking.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned looking at your wrist first. Was the vibration distracting?

**Participant:** Not really during the test. But in a real gallery, if it happened every time I walked near a painting, yeah, that might get annoying. Especially if I'm already talking to someone.

**Interviewer:** Do you normally go to galleries with other people?

**Participant:** Usually with a friend. We don't talk about every artwork, but if something's interesting we'll stop and argue about what it means. In a good way. [laughs]

**Interviewer:** How would the Echoes fit into that sort of visit?

**Participant:** I can imagine saying, "Oh, people felt really differently about this one," and then showing my friend. That could start a conversation. But I'd want to make my own choice before seeing everyone else's colours.

**Interviewer:** Why is that order important to you?

**Participant:** Because I change my mind pretty easily. If I saw a lot of people choosing one colour, I'd probably wonder if I got it wrong, even though there isn't a wrong answer.

**Interviewer:** Did any part of the prototype make you return to the artwork itself?

**Participant:** The "Look Again" bit did. I noticed the background after that. At first I'd only been looking at the main figure. That was nice because it didn't tell me what to think, just to look another time.

**Interviewer:** How was it to wear and use while standing in front of the painting?

**Participant:** The band itself was okay. The hand movement felt a little obvious, though. I was imagining doing it in a busy room with people walking past. I wouldn't want to stand with my palm open for ages.

**Interviewer:** Would that stop you using it?

**Participant:** No, not completely. I'd use it for paintings I was already interested in. I just wouldn't want to do the whole process for every single one.

**Interviewer:** If we changed one thing before another test, what should it be?

**Participant:** Make it really clear when your Echo is submitted. And maybe explain the colour idea in one sentence at the start. After that, leave people alone to explore.

## Closing

**Interviewer:** That's really useful. Is there anything I didn't ask about that you wanted to mention?

**Participant:** Just that I like the idea more as a conversation starter than as a guide. I wouldn't want it telling me how to look at every painting.

**Interviewer:** Got it. Thanks for taking the time.

**Participant:** No worries.
