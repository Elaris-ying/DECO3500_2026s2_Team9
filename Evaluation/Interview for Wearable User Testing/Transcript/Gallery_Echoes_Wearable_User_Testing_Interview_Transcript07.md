# Participant 2 — Interview Record

## 1. First Impression

### Q: What do you think this wearable device is designed to do?

**Participant Response:**  
I think it is a way to express how a painting makes me feel using light. When I choose a colour on the app to represent my impression of a painting, the light in my palm changes to the same colour before it is submitted to the collective artwork.

### Q: Was its purpose clear to you at first?

**Participant Response:**  
Pretty quickly, yes. Once I saw the colour on the phone match the light in my palm, I understood that it was turning my personal impression into a visible colour response.

### Q: Was there anything that confused you?

**Participant Response:**  
At first, I was not sure whether the light only showed my own colour or whether it would also react when other visitors submitted their colours.

### Q: Without explanation, would you know what to do next?

**Participant Response:**  
Once the app opened, yes. The screen asked me to choose a colour for my impression, so I selected one, checked the light in my palm, and then submitted it.

---

## 2. Wearability

### Q: How did the wearable device feel when you were wearing it?

**Participant Response:**  
It felt alright. It was like wearing an adjustable band with a small light module in the palm. It did not really get in the way while walking around.

### Q: Did it feel comfortable?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, it was light enough. The strap also felt secure, so I did not worry about it slipping while I was holding my phone.

### Q: Did it feel too large, heavy, or distracting?

**Participant Response:**  
It was not heavy, but having something across my palm meant that I could not fully close my hand or put that hand deeply into my pocket.

### Q: Would you feel comfortable wearing it during a gallery visit?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, I think it would be fine for a normal gallery visit. One hand can hold the phone for choosing colours, while the other hand shows the light.

### Q: Would you prefer the device to be more visible or more subtle?

**Participant Response:**  
I would prefer it to be more subtle. A soft and gentle light would fit the quiet gallery environment better than a very bright or sharp light.

---

## 3. Palm Interaction

### Q: How natural did it feel to interact with the interface on your palm?

**Participant Response:**  
It felt very intuitive. Choosing the colour on the phone and immediately seeing the same colour appear in my palm made the digital action feel more physical.

### Q: Was it easy to understand how to choose a colour and view an Echo?

**Participant Response:**  
Choosing the impression colour was very simple. The collective Echo took a little longer to understand, but the visual result still made sense.

### Q: Which part felt the most natural?

**Participant Response:**  
The real-time colour change felt the most natural. When I moved my finger across the colour wheel, the light in my palm changed at the same time without any obvious delay.

### Q: Which part felt awkward or unclear?

**Participant Response:**  
I had to keep my palm open and facing toward me to see the light clearly, while also trying to look at the artwork. That made the posture feel a little awkward.

### Q: Would you prefer this interaction or using a phone? Why?

**Participant Response:**  
I like having the light in my palm. Using only a phone feels like a normal app, but seeing the colour appear physically in my hand makes the experience feel more connected to the space.

---

## 4. Attention to the Artwork

### Q: Did the wearable help you stay focused on the artwork, or did it distract you?

**Participant Response:**  
After the initial novelty wore off, I think it helped. Thinking about which colour best represented my impression of the painting made me stop and look at it for longer.

### Q: Which part of the interaction distracted you the most?

**Participant Response:**  
Choosing the exact shade on the phone was the most distracting part. When I focused too much on finding the right colour, I stopped looking at the artwork for a moment.

### Q: Did the vibration feel helpful or annoying?

**Participant Response:**  
It was a useful reminder. If I was just walking around without really paying attention, a small vibration could remind me to stop and look around.

### Q: Did the “Look Again” prompt make you look at the artwork differently?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes. I do not have an art background, so I usually just look at the main subject. The prompt made me look again and notice more of the overall mood and background.

### Q: How long do you think each interaction should take?

**Participant Response:**  
It should be quite short, maybe around fifteen seconds. I would choose a colour, check the light, submit it, and then continue looking at the artwork.

---

## 5. Echoes and Social Awareness

### Q: How did seeing other visitors’ Echoes affect your experience?

**Participant Response:**  
It was interesting. Seeing different visitors’ colours together showed me how differently people can respond to the same artwork.

### Q: Did the Echoes help you notice something new about the artwork?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes. For example, if someone chose a bright yellow for a darker painting, it might make me look more closely and try to understand why they saw something warm in it.

### Q: Would you want to see other visitors’ Echoes? Why or why not?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, because it is anonymous and very visual. Long written comments can feel tiring, but colours are quick and easy to understand.

### Q: Did other people’s Echoes influence your own interpretation?

**Participant Response:**  
They gave me another perspective. They did not change my first impression, but they helped me realise that there could be another way to understand the artwork.

### Q: Does this feel less stressful than talking directly to strangers?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, much less stressful. I do not know much art vocabulary, so discussing artworks with strangers could feel uncomfortable. Sharing a colour feels much easier.

---

## 6. Choice and Control

### Q: Did you feel that you had enough control while using the device?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes. I could decide whether I wanted to submit a colour or simply continue walking without interacting.

### Q: Would you like to be able to skip or ignore an Echo?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes. Sometimes I would prefer to have a quiet moment and not see what other people thought.

### Q: Is it important for you to be able to turn off or ignore the vibration?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, definitely. If I was already focused on an artwork, I would want the option to mute the vibration so it would not interrupt me.

### Q: Would you prefer the system to give suggestions automatically, or would you rather choose when to interact?

**Participant Response:**  
I would rather choose when to interact. A small reminder is fine, but I still want to decide when I submit my own response.

### Q: Did any part of the experience feel forced?

**Participant Response:**  
Not really. However, if I felt that I had to choose a colour for every artwork, it could start to feel like a checklist. I think it works better when the interaction is completely optional.

---

## 7. Overall Experience

### Q: If this device were available in a real gallery, would you use it? Why or why not?

**Participant Response:**  
Yes, I would. It gives normal visitors like me a simple way to take part in the gallery experience without needing any special knowledge about art.

### Q: What was the most useful or interesting feature?

**Participant Response:**  
The most interesting part was choosing a colour on the phone, seeing the same colour appear in my palm, and then sending that colour into the collective artwork. The whole process felt connected.

### Q: Was there any feature you felt was unnecessary?

**Participant Response:**  
The vibration felt slightly unnecessary. The light changing in my palm was already enough to get my attention.

### Q: What would you change about the experience?

**Participant Response:**  
I would make the light softer and more spread out, so it creates a gentle glow on the hand instead of looking like one strong light point.

### Q: If you could improve only one thing, what would it be?

**Participant Response:**  
I would make the palm light easier to notice from a normal angle, so I would not need to keep looking down and holding my hand completely flat.
