# Gallery Echoes — Wearable User Testing Interview Transcript 07

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for helping us test Gallery Echoes. I'd like to hear your first impressions while they're still fresh. Is it alright to audio-record the interview? We won't attach your name to the notes.

**Participant:** Yeah, sure.

**Interviewer:** Thanks. Could you start by telling me what you normally enjoy about going to an art gallery?

**Participant:** I tend to go alone, actually. I like being able to take my time. Sometimes I stand in front of one work for a while and sometimes I just walk past. I don't really plan it.

## Conversation

**Interviewer:** Thinking about that, where did the wearable fit comfortably into your experience, and where didn't it?

**Participant:** I liked not having to take out my phone. That part makes sense. But the first vibration made me feel I was supposed to do something immediately, and I didn't love that.

**Interviewer:** What did you do when it vibrated?

**Participant:** I ignored it at first. I was still looking at the painting. Then I tried the palm interface after a few seconds. I guess I wanted to finish my own thought first.

**Interviewer:** Was there anything about the interaction itself that was difficult?

**Participant:** The gesture was easy enough once I'd seen it demonstrated. I wouldn't call it completely natural, though. You have to move from looking outward at the art to looking down at your hand. It's a small interruption, but it's still an interruption.

**Interviewer:** How did you find the colour choices?

**Participant:** I'm a little torn. I appreciate that there wasn't loads of text, because I wouldn't read a paragraph while standing there. But a colour can mean different things. I chose one because it matched the feeling of the painting, not necessarily my emotion.

**Interviewer:** Did that feel like a problem?

**Participant:** Not for me, as long as the system doesn't pretend the colour has one exact meaning. If it said, "You are feeling sad," I'd disagree. If it just said, "This is your Echo," I'd be okay with it.

**Interviewer:** What was it like seeing Echoes from other visitors?

**Participant:** Interesting, but I didn't immediately feel connected to those people. It was more like, oh, there are other interpretations here. That's still useful. I just wouldn't describe it as a social experience in the normal sense.

**Interviewer:** Did seeing them change what you noticed?

**Participant:** A little. I went back to check the darker part of the artwork, because the different colours made me think I'd missed something. Although, to be fair, I might have looked again anyway.

**Interviewer:** That's fair. What did you think about the "Look Again" prompt?

**Participant:** I understood what it was trying to do. I liked it more after the Echoes than before them. If it appeared too early, it could feel like an instruction from a teacher. After seeing another interpretation, it felt like an invitation.

**Interviewer:** How did the wristband feel physically?

**Participant:** Fine for a few minutes. I did notice it when I bent my wrist, but it wasn't painful or anything. I'd want to try it for longer before saying I could wear it for a full exhibition.

**Interviewer:** If you were visiting on your own next weekend, would you choose to use this?

**Participant:** Maybe for part of the visit. I'd be happy to try it at a few artworks. I wouldn't wear it if I had to respond each time it vibrated.

**Interviewer:** What would make that choice easier?

**Participant:** Knowing I could just ignore it without missing something important. And having some way to keep it quiet for a while. The gallery should still feel like my own experience.

## Wrap-up

**Interviewer:** One last question: what should we absolutely keep?

**Participant:** Keep the short interaction and the option to see different reactions. Just don't make participation feel compulsory.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That's a helpful distinction.

**Participant:** Sure, happy to help.
