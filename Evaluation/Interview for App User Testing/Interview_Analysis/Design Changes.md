# Evaluation Findings and Design Changes

This document summarises the main findings from the prototype evaluation and explains how these findings informed the next design iteration of Gallery Echoes.

The findings are based on the six groups developed from the interview initial coding. Some parts of Prototype 1 were retained because participants understood and valued them, while other parts were simplified or redesigned based on the evaluation results.

---

## 1. Retain Personal Responses to Artworks

**Based on:** G1 Personal Responses to Artworks

### Finding

Participants generally understood Gallery Echoes as a way to express their own feelings and responses to artworks while also seeing how other visitors responded to the same artwork.

The idea of leaving a personal response was understood as part of the gallery experience rather than as a traditional information guide.

### Design Direction

- Keep personal responses as a core part of Gallery Echoes.
- Allow visitors to express their emotional response to an artwork.
- Connect individual responses to a shared visitor experience.
- Keep the interaction focused on personal interpretation rather than giving visitors a fixed interpretation of the artwork.

### Rationale

The evaluation showed that the basic idea of personal response was understood by participants. Therefore, this part of the concept does not need to be replaced, but the way visitors express and share their responses can be refined in Prototype 2.

---

## 2. Simplify the Interaction Flow

**Based on:** G2 Interaction Flow and Clarity

### Finding

Participants generally understood the main interaction flow, but some steps, labels, and transitions were unclear.

Several participants were unsure about what to do after tapping the NFC tag, whether they needed to submit something before continuing, or what certain buttons meant. Participants also felt that some screens and steps could be combined.

### Design Change

- Reduce the number of steps in the main interaction.
- Combine screens where possible.
- Make the next action more obvious.
- Add short instructions when needed.
- Simplify unclear labels such as “Add a Thought”, “Share Feelings”, and “Share Artwork”.
- Keep response methods quick and easy to understand.

### Rationale

The overall interaction structure was understood, so a complete redesign of the user flow is not necessary. The main problem is the number and clarity of individual steps. Prototype 2 should therefore keep the basic flow while making it shorter and clearer.

---

## 3. Make the Collective Visual More Understandable

**Based on:** G3 Understanding the Collective Visual

### Finding

Participants generally understood that the collective visual represented responses from different visitors. They also liked the idea that many individual responses could become part of one shared visual.

However, some participants were unsure about what specific colours, shapes, or visual elements represented. Some also wanted to understand how their own response contributed to the final collective visual.

### Design Change

- Strengthen the connection between visitor input and visual output.
- Let visitors clearly see their own contribution appear in the collective artwork.
- Use colour to represent the visitor's emotional response.
- Use the size of the contribution to represent emotional intensity.
- Generate each contribution as an individual organic ink-drop form.
- Allow individual contributions to overlap and gradually build the collective artwork.
- Use visual influence from the original artwork to help determine where contributions appear.

### Rationale

The evaluation supports keeping the collective visual because participants understood and valued the idea of combining individual responses.

However, the relationship between personal input and the final visual needs to be clearer. Prototype 2 will therefore make each contribution more visible and give the visual system more understandable rules.

The specific use of colour, size, ink-drop forms, and spatial placement is a design decision developed by the team in response to this finding.

---

## 4. Simplify Social Interaction Around Visitor Sketches

**Based on:** G4 Social Connection Through Visitor Responses

### Finding

Participants enjoyed seeing how other visitors responded to the same artwork. Other visitors' sketches could provide different perspectives, inspiration, and a sense of social connection.

At the same time, participants preferred the social interaction to remain simple and lightweight. Some participants did not want the experience to become similar to a social media platform with too many comments, profiles, followers, or other social features.

### Design Change

- Remove the feature for sharing written thoughts or reflections with other visitors.
- Keep the option for visitors to upload their own sketches of artworks.
- Allow visitors to view sketches uploaded by other visitors.
- Allow visitors to like other visitors' sketches.
- Remove commenting.
- Remove dislike functions.
- Avoid profiles, followers, and other complex social features.
- Keep sketch sharing simple and low-pressure.

### Rationale

The purpose of the social feature is to let visitors discover different perspectives without turning Gallery Echoes into a social media platform.

Sketches provide a visual way for visitors to share what they noticed or how they interpreted an artwork. Keeping only a simple like interaction allows visitors to acknowledge another person's contribution without creating additional pressure to comment or participate in extended social interaction.

Removing written reflection sharing also reduces overlap between different parts of the experience. Emotional responses can be represented through the collective visual, while sketches provide a separate form of visitor-created content.

---

## 5. Keep the Experience Artwork-Focused

**Based on:** G5 Keeping the Experience Artwork-Focused

### Finding

Participants did not want to spend too much time looking at their phones or screens while standing in front of physical artworks.

Participants also showed different preferences about when they wanted to see other visitors' responses. Some preferred to form their own interpretation first, while others felt more comfortable after seeing what other visitors had shared.

### Design Change

- Reduce unnecessary screen time.
- Keep digital interactions short.
- Avoid displaying too much visitor-generated content at once.
- Make other visitors' content optional rather than forcing it into the main interaction flow.
- Allow visitors to focus on the artwork before opening shared visitor content.
- Keep the main artwork experience separate from optional social exploration.

### Rationale

Gallery Echoes should support engagement with physical artworks rather than compete for visitors' attention.

Because participants had different preferences about when to view other people's responses, Prototype 2 should give visitors more control over whether and when they access shared visitor content.

This keeps the digital experience flexible while protecting the visitor's own interpretation of the artwork.

---

## 6. Keep Gallery Support Features Simple

**Based on:** G6 Supporting the Gallery Visit

### Finding

Participants found NFC useful because it provided quick access to information connected to a specific artwork.

Some participants also valued artwork information and visit tracking because these features could help them understand artworks or remember where they had already been. However, these supporting features were not equally important to every participant.

### Design Change

- Keep NFC as the main physical entry point to the digital experience.
- Make artwork information available quickly after tapping the NFC tag.
- Reduce unnecessary screens between the NFC interaction and artwork information.
- Keep the gallery map and visit tracker simple.
- Keep navigation and tracking features secondary to the artwork and social experience.
- Avoid adding unnecessary functions that make the gallery experience more complex.

### Rationale

NFC provides a simple connection between the physical artwork and the digital experience without requiring visitors to manually search for an artwork.

Artwork information, maps, and visit tracking can support the gallery visit, but they should remain supporting functions rather than becoming the main focus of Gallery Echoes.

---

# Summary of Design Changes

The Prototype 2 direction focuses on simplifying the experience while strengthening the relationship between individual visitors, artworks, and shared visitor contributions.

The main changes are:

1. Retain personal emotional responses as a core interaction.
2. Simplify the overall interaction flow and improve interface clarity.
3. Redesign the collective visual so individual contributions and their meanings are clearer.
4. Remove written reflection sharing and focus social interaction on visitor sketches with likes only.
5. Reduce digital distraction and give visitors more control over when they view other people's contributions.
6. Keep NFC, artwork information, maps, and visit tracking as simple supporting features.

These changes maintain the core idea of Gallery Echoes while reducing unnecessary interaction and making the shared visitor experience clearer and more suitable for a gallery environment.
