# Gallery Echoes Interview Transcript — Naturalised Version

**Interviewer:** Can you briefly describe what you think Gallery Echoes is for, in your own words?

**Participant:** I think it’s mainly there to help you reflect on the artwork a bit, and also see what other people thought about it. It feels less like an audio guide and more like something you use to leave a quick reaction.

**Interviewer:** So it’s less about giving information and more about sharing responses?

**Participant:** Yeah, I think so. It’s more like, “What did you notice?” or “How did this make you feel?” rather than telling you what the artwork means.

---

**Interviewer:** Was there any point during the experience where you were unsure what to do next? What happened?

**Participant:** Yeah, a little bit between P1 and P2. On P1 there was the “Add a thought” button, and then P2 showed the time I’d spent with the artwork and another button saying “Share Feelings”. I wasn’t sure if P2 was supposed to appear automatically or if I’d done something to trigger it.

And on P4, the button saying “Share artwork” confused me for a second. I thought it might open the normal phone sharing menu, like sending it to someone, rather than sharing my response in the app.

**Interviewer:** Did that stop you from continuing?

**Participant:** No, I still tapped it. I just paused for a second because I wasn’t completely sure what would happen.

---

**Interviewer:** After tapping the NFC tag, did the steps from viewing the artwork to sharing your feelings make sense to you?

**Participant:** Mostly, yeah. I understood the overall flow. You tap the tag, look at the artwork, choose how you feel, and then leave a response.

I liked P3 because the question “What caught your eye in this artwork?” is pretty easy to answer. And the mood tags help if you don’t really want to type much.

I think P1, P2 and P3 could probably be shorter though. It felt like a few steps before I actually got to write anything.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer going straight to the reflection page?

**Participant:** Maybe not straight away, but I think P1 and P2 could probably be combined. Like, show the artwork name, how long I’ve been there, and the prompt on the same page.

---

**Interviewer:** What did you think the collective visual represented?

**Participant:** I thought each dot was probably another visitor’s response, and the colours showed different moods. So maybe blue means calm, yellow means curious, something like that.

When my dot appeared, it was kind of nice because you could see that other people had also reacted to the same artwork.

**Interviewer:** Did that make you feel more connected to other visitors?

**Participant:** A little bit, yeah. You don’t really talk to strangers in galleries, so it was interesting to see that other people had felt something too.

---

**Interviewer:** How did you feel about seeing and interacting with other visitors’ reflections and sketches?

**Participant:** I liked that part. The comments were short, so I could read them quickly without standing there on my phone for ages.

I also liked that the sketches looked pretty casual. They didn’t look like professional drawings or anything, so it made it feel less intimidating.

**Interviewer:** Did the anonymous posting help?

**Participant:** Yeah. I think if names and profiles were attached, I’d probably think about it more before posting. Anonymous feels easier.

**Interviewer:** Would you be tempted to draw and share a sketch yourself?

**Participant:** Probably, yeah. Especially if I could see that other people were just doing quick sketches too. I wouldn’t feel like it had to look good.

---

**Interviewer:** If you were actually visiting a gallery, which feature would you be most likely to use again?

**Participant:** Probably the map and checklist, P5 to P8. I liked being able to see what I’d already visited and what was still left.

Sometimes in galleries I forget which rooms I’ve already been through, so that would actually be useful.

**Interviewer:** What would you change or remove?

**Participant:** I’d probably combine P1 and P2 so there are fewer taps.

And I’m not sure about the “Optional: add a photo” part on P3. If I’m already standing in front of the artwork, I probably wouldn’t want to stop and take another photo or look through my camera roll.

**Interviewer:** Why not?

**Participant:** I think it would distract me a bit. I’d rather look at the actual artwork than spend too much time doing things on my phone.
