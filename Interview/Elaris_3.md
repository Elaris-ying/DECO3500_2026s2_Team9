# Interview Response – Parent Visiting with Family

## 1. Can you tell me about the last time you visited an art gallery? Who were you with, and what did you do during the visit?

I went with my family, mainly because I wanted to take my child somewhere different on the weekend. We walked around the exhibitions and talked about some of the artworks. I didn't really understand or like a lot of them, but my child seemed interested, so I mostly followed them around.

We also took some photos and stopped to rest a few times.

## 2. Can you remember a moment during the visit when something made you stop for longer or pay more attention? What were you doing at that moment?

There was an area where visitors could sit down and draw. My child wanted to try it, so we stayed there for quite a while.

I wasn't paying that much attention to the artwork before, but when my child started drawing it, I looked at the original more carefully too. We talked about the colours and some small details in the picture.

## 3. When you visit with friends or other people, how does being with them affect the way you experience the gallery? Can you give an example?

For me, being with my family is probably the main reason I enjoy the visit.

Sometimes I don't find an artwork very interesting, but if my child gets excited about it or asks me a question, I'll stop and look at it with them. I probably wouldn't do that if I was visiting alone.

## 4. Can you think of a time when another visitor's behaviour, comment, or activity changed what you did or noticed in the gallery?

Yes. We saw some other visitors sitting and sketching, and my child noticed them first. After seeing them, my child wanted to draw too, so we went over to the drawing area.

I probably wouldn't have noticed that activity if other people weren't already doing it.

## 5. Are there moments when you enjoy sharing the experience with other people, and moments when you prefer to explore the gallery by yourself? Why?

I prefer sharing the experience with my family. I'm not really someone who would go to a gallery alone.

I enjoy talking about what we see, especially when my child has a completely different opinion from me. Sometimes they really like something that I don't find attractive at all, which can be quite funny.

## 6. Thinking back on the visit, was there anything that made the experience especially enjoyable, uncomfortable, tiring, or memorable?

The most enjoyable part was seeing my child having fun, especially when they could actually do something instead of just looking at artworks.

The tiring part was walking around and sometimes not knowing where to go next. We went through some of the same areas more than once because we weren't sure if we had seen everything.

I think activities and clearer directions would make the visit better for families like us.
