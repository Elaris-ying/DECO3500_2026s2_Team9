### 1. Can you tell me about the last time you visited an art gallery? Who were you with, and what did you do during the visit?
**Possible follow-up:** Did you take photos, sketch, talk, rest, or do anything else besides looking at artworks?

**Answer:**
My most recent gallery visit was on May 2 this year. I went with a friend and we happened to come across a small solo sculpture exhibition called *Cang Hai Cuo*. It was raining heavily that day and we had both been caught in the rain, so we bought milk tea and drank it while walking through the exhibition. We spent the visit looking at the sculptures and learning about the stories and ideas behind them.

### 2. Can you remember a moment during the visit when something made you stop for longer or pay more attention? What were you doing at that moment?

**Answer:**
The exhibition was based on a series of original mythological stories created by the artist. I found it particularly interesting to read the background stories first and then examine how different elements of those stories were represented in the sculptures. Because the mythology was created by a contemporary artist, it included modern concepts and expressions such as “getting rich,” “the banking industry,” and “the hotel industry.” The combination of mythological storytelling with these very modern ideas was unusual and made me pay much closer attention to the details of the works.

### 3. When you visit with friends or other people, how does being with them affect the way you experience the gallery? Can you give an example?

**Answer:**
Visiting with friends can give me perspectives that are different from my own. The friend who visited this exhibition with me is an illustrator, so they had a much deeper understanding of elements such as character design and the use of colour. I am usually more interested in the cultural ideas and stories behind artworks. As a result, discussing the exhibition together allowed us to view the same works from different perspectives and gave me a more multidimensional understanding of the exhibition.

### 4. Can you think of a time when another visitor’s behavior, comment, or activity changed what you did or noticed in the gallery?

**Answer:**
The exhibition was based on the folk culture of coastal communities. Since I come from an inland area, I was not familiar with many of the cultural stories behind it. During the visit, I overheard another visitor talking about the importance of the ocean to people living in coastal communities. Their comments helped me understand why deities associated with the sea are often represented from two contrasting perspectives. On one hand, the sea is generous because it provides coastal communities with resources and prosperity. On the other hand, it is mysterious and unpredictable, and can bring disasters without warning. Understanding this contrast gave me a much deeper appreciation of the exhibition and the ideas behind the artworks.

### 5. Are there moments when you enjoy sharing the experience with other people, and moments when you prefer to explore the gallery by yourself? Why?

**Answer:**
I think visiting an exhibition alone allows me to become more immersed in its atmosphere and gives me more space for personal reflection. In contrast, visiting with other people is useful because discussions can introduce new perspectives and ideas that I might not have considered myself. Personally, I prefer visiting exhibitions alone and reflecting on or summarising my thoughts afterwards. However, if I visit with a group, I usually prefer to explore the exhibition independently first and then discuss it with the others afterwards.

### 6. Thinking back on the visit, was there anything that made the experience especially enjoyable, uncomfortable, tiring, or memorable?
**Possible follow-up:** Was it related to the space, people, activities, or something else?

**Answer:**
There was nothing particularly unpleasant about the overall experience, although the heavy rain caused some practical difficulties. The exhibition space was relatively small, and because many visitors entered after being outside in the rain, some areas of the floor became slippery. This was mainly an inconvenience caused by the weather rather than the exhibition itself. Another issue was that there was no guide or guided explanation available, so I mainly relied on the written information provided alongside the works. However, the text was designed in a very small font, which made some of the exhibition information difficult to read.
