**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time you visited an art gallery? Who were you with, and what did you do during the visit?

**Participant:** Yeah, it was on May 2 this year. I went with a friend, and we... we actually came across this small solo sculpture exhibition by chance. It was called *Cang Hai Cuo*.

That day it was raining really heavily and we'd already been caught in the rain, so... yeah, we were both a bit wet. We bought some milk tea and basically walked around the exhibition while drinking it. We looked at the sculptures and also read some of the stories and information around them.

**Interviewer:** Can you remember a moment during the visit when something made you stop for longer or pay more attention? What were you doing at that moment?

**Participant:** Hmm... I think, I think what interested me most was actually the story behind the exhibition.

The artist had created this whole series of original mythological stories, so I would read the background first and then... then go back to the sculpture and look at the design again. And that was quite interesting because you could start connecting the story with the visual details.

And because the myths were created by a modern person, there were some really modern words in them. Things like “getting rich,” “banking,” and “the hotel industry.” So it was... it was kind of strange, but in an interesting way. You have this mythological world, but then suddenly there are these very modern ideas inside it.

I think that contrast made me spend more time looking at the sculptures.

**Interviewer:** When you visit with friends or other people, how does being with them affect the way you experience the gallery? Can you give an example?

**Participant:** I think going with someone else gives you... gives you another way of looking at the same thing.

The friend who went with me that day is an illustrator, so they know much more about things like character design, colour, and... visual details in general. I'm usually more interested in the cultural background or the story behind the work.

So, yeah, when we talked about the same sculpture, we were actually focusing on very different things. They might talk about the colour or the design of a character, and I might be thinking more about what that character represents.

I don't know, I think it made the experience more... more complete, maybe? Because I wasn't only looking at it from my own perspective.

**Interviewer:** Can you think of a time when another visitor's behavior, comment, or activity changed what you did or noticed in the gallery?

**Participant:** Yeah, actually... that happened during this exhibition.

The exhibition was based on folk culture from coastal cities, but I'm from an inland area, so I, I really don't know much about coastal culture. A lot of the background was quite unfamiliar to me.

And then I overheard another visitor talking about how important the ocean is to people living on the coast. That made me think about it differently.

Because... the ocean has these two sides, right? On one side, it's generous. It gives people food, resources, wealth... things like that. But on the other side, it's also really unpredictable. It can bring disasters, and people don't always know when something bad is going to happen.

So then I started to understand why sea-related gods in the exhibition also seemed to have these two different sides. Before that, I hadn't really... I hadn't made that connection. After hearing that person talk about it, the exhibition made much more sense to me.

**Interviewer:** Are there moments when you enjoy sharing the experience with other people, and moments when you prefer to explore the gallery by yourself? Why?

**Participant:** Hmm... personally, I think I still prefer visiting exhibitions alone.

When I'm alone, I can... I can get into the atmosphere more easily, I think. And I have more time to think about things by myself without immediately talking about them.

But at the same time, going with other people is useful because... well, like I said before, other people notice different things. So discussion can give me new ideas.

Usually, if I go with a group, I actually prefer to look around by myself first. Like, everyone can just go and see what they want to see. And then afterwards we can meet again and talk about it. I think that works better for me than discussing everything while I'm still looking.

**Interviewer:** Thinking back on the visit, was there anything that made the experience especially enjoyable, uncomfortable, tiring, or memorable?

**Participant:** There wasn't anything really unpleasant, I don't think. The main problem was probably just the weather.

It was raining very heavily that day, and the exhibition space wasn't very big, so... because everyone was coming in from outside, the floor became a little slippery in some places. But that was, that was more because of the rain than the exhibition itself.

And another thing was the written information. There wasn't a guide, so you had to rely quite a lot on the text next to the works.

But the font was... it was really small. Really, really small. So sometimes I had to get quite close to read it, and even then it wasn't very easy. I think that was probably the main design issue I noticed.
