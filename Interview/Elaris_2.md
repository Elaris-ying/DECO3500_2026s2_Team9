# Gallery Visitor Interview Transcript

**Participant Profile:** Casual visitor, visited alone on a whim, tried sketching with gallery-provided pencils.

---

### 1. Can you tell me about the last time you visited an art gallery? Who were you with, and what did you do during the visit?

"I went to a gallery in the city last weekend. It was totally spontaneous—I just had a free afternoon and wanted to kill some time somewhere quiet. I went alone. Besides just walking around looking at the paintings, I saw they had a little sketching area with some paper and basic pencils. So I grabbed a pencil, sat on a bench, and spent maybe twenty minutes trying to draw a statue. I also took a couple of quick phone photos of some artwork labels to look up later."

---

### 2. Can you remember a moment during the visit when something made you stop for longer or pay more attention? What were you doing at that moment?

"Yeah, there was this big landscape painting. I sat down with my pencil and tried to copy the shadows under the trees. When you actually try to draw something yourself, you start looking way closer. I realised the dark areas weren't just flat dark patches—there were so many subtle strokes and layers. Trying to sketch it made me slow down and notice all the little details I'd normally just walk right past."

---

### 3. When you visit with friends or other people, how does being with them affect the way you experience the gallery? Can you give an example?

"When I'm with friends, it's way more about chatting and having fun, but it's harder to focus. If I'm by myself, I can easily zone out for twenty minutes just messing around with a pencil. But with friends, we're usually joking around, talking about what a weird sculpture means, or taking photos. It turns into more of a social hangout than a quiet personal experience."

---

### 4. Can you think of a time when another visitor's behaviour, comment, or activity changed what you did or noticed in the gallery?

"Definitely. While I was trying to sketch that statue, another visitor sat down nearby and started drawing too. Her sketch looked super clean and confident, while mine was pretty clumsy. Since the gallery literally only gave us pencils—no erasers at all—I couldn't fix any of my mistakes. I noticed she was pressing really lightly, just building up faint layers instead of drawing hard lines. Seeing that made me realise how heavy-handed I was, and I really wanted to turn around and ask her for some tips, but I felt a bit shy."

---

### 5. Are there moments when you enjoy sharing the experience with other people, and moments when you prefer to explore the gallery by yourself? Why?

"I prefer being alone when I'm just walking through the rooms, because I can go at my own pace without waiting for anyone. But right after I finished that sketch, I really wished I had someone to talk to. My drawing was honestly pretty mediocre, but I still wanted to show it to someone, laugh about how hard it was without an eraser, and see how they would draw it. Doing something hands-on makes you want to share and connect."

---

### 6. Thinking back on the visit, was there anything that made the experience especially enjoyable, uncomfortable, tiring, or memorable?

"The best part was definitely the sketching activity. Even though my drawing wasn't great and there wasn't even an eraser to fix bad lines, physically trying to draw made the artwork stick in my head way more. On the other hand, the wooden bench didn't have a backrest, so leaning forward and sketching for twenty minutes made my back and neck pretty stiff. But overall, that little pencil activity made the whole trip feel a lot more fun and memorable."
