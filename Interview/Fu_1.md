**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time you visited an art gallery? Who were you with, and what did you do during the visit?

**Participant:** Yeah... I think it was a few months ago. I went with a friend to a free art museum in the city. We, we mostly just walked around and looked at the paintings and installations. We took some photos too, and... there was this kind of “painting together” activity, so we joined that as well. It was, yeah, it was quite fun.

**Interviewer:** Can you remember a moment during the visit when something made you stop for longer or pay more attention? What were you doing at that moment?

**Participant:** Hmm... yeah, there were a few paintings that I thought were really, really well done. So I stopped for longer and looked at them more closely. I was looking at the brushstrokes, and the expressions of the people in the paintings, and... just small details, I guess. Sometimes I would kind of move closer because I wanted to see exactly how something had been painted.

**Interviewer:** When you visit with friends or other people, how does being with them affect the way you experience the gallery? Can you give an example?

**Participant:** I think it definitely changes how long I stay in different areas. Like, if my friend isn't really interested in one gallery or one section, then we, we probably just look around quickly and move on. We don't spend much time there. But if they're really interested in something, then I'll stay with them and... maybe ask why they like it. And then I usually start looking at it more carefully too.

**Interviewer:** Can you think of a time when another visitor's behavior, comment, or activity changed what you did or noticed in the gallery?

**Participant:** Yeah... if my friend says, like, “Oh, this painting is really good,” then I'll usually make a point of looking at it again. Because maybe I didn't notice whatever they noticed the first time.

And... I also pay attention to other visitors sometimes. Especially if someone is doing something disrespectful to the artwork. Like touching an installation, or, you know, damaging something. That kind of behaviour really catches my attention.

**Interviewer:** Are there moments when you enjoy sharing the experience with other people, and moments when you prefer to explore the gallery by yourself? Why?

**Participant:** I mostly, I mostly prefer going with other people because there's always something to talk about. You can discuss what you liked or... what you didn't really understand.

But going alone is good too. I think when I'm alone, I can look at things in more detail and just stay there for as long as I want. I don't need to think about whether someone else wants to move on. But... I don't know, afterwards it can feel a little empty, maybe, because there's no one there to immediately talk to about what I saw.

**Interviewer:** Thinking back on the visit, was there anything that made the experience especially enjoyable, uncomfortable, tiring, or memorable?

**Participant:** Hmm... there were some projection areas that were completely dark. Like, really dark. I didn't feel very comfortable in those spaces. I don't know, maybe it was just the atmosphere, but... yeah, I wanted to leave those areas quite quickly.
